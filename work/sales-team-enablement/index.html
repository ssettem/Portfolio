Sales Team Enablement Analytics

PROCESS DESIGN · TRAINING
Think & Learn (Byju’s) · Jul 2020 – Mar 2022

“Enablement” is the unglamorous work behind a sales floor that performs consistently: turning what your best reps do instinctively into something a new hire on any of your teams can be taught. I wrote and rolled out that documentation across 40+ associates spanning 10 teams at Byju’s.

The problem with tribal knowledge

At 10 teams and 40+ associates, ramp quality depended entirely on which manager trained you and how good they were at explaining what they did without thinking about it. A top performer’s objection-handling and qualification instincts lived in their head, not on paper, so it never reached the next hire.

Illustrative comparison: how a new associate ramped, before and after
Before: tribal knowledge
Ramp quality set by whichever manager trained you. Objection handling, qualification criteria, and call structure varied team to team, undocumented.
→
After: standardized playbook
One documented sales playbook and onboarding doc set, the same ramp path and qualification standard for every associate, on every team.
Illustrates the structural change the playbooks made, not a measured before/after metric, ramp-time and conversion figures weren’t tracked at the individual level.

What I actually built

01

Turning instinct into something teachable

I sat with the highest-converting associates to pull out their actual call structure, objection responses, and qualification checkpoints, then wrote it down as a script any team could run, not just the reps who’d figured it out on their own.

02

No more onboarding by luck of the draw

New hires used to learn by shadowing whoever happened to be free. I wrote a standard onboarding sequence so ramp quality stopped depending on which manager or team someone landed on.

03

Rolling it out, then actually keeping it current

I delivered the playbook and onboarding docs to 40+ associates myself, then kept revising them based on what managers said wasn’t landing in real calls.

Scope

40+
Associates trained on the standardized playbook
10
Teams operating on one shared process

Byju’s reported shorter ramp time and higher conversion among teams using the standardized materials, tracked qualitatively by managers rather than a per-associate metric, so it’s described here directionally rather than as a specific figure.
