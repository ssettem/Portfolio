BRD & Gap Analysis at Radiance

REQUIREMENTS · REPORTING AUTOMATION
Radiance · Nov 2023 – Aug 2024

A BRD, Business Requirements Document, is the written translation between what a client asks for and what a delivery team actually builds. A gap analysis is the check that comes after: does what shipped match what was signed off? Across 35+ software projects for 17+ clients, that check was my job, and so was closing the gaps it found before launch instead of after.

What a “gap” looks like

Requirements get lost in translation between a client meeting and a sprint board. A gap analysis means putting the two side by side, what was actually requested against what the build produced, and catching the mismatch before a client finds it in a demo.

Illustrative example: the kind of mismatch a gap analysis catches
Requested (BRD)
Expense approvals over $500 require sign-off from both the manager and finance.
→
Delivered (build)
Workflow only routes to the manager. Finance sign-off step is missing.
GAP FLAGGED: breaks an audit control if it ships
✓ Caught in gap analysis, finance step added before go-live
A representative example of the requested-vs-delivered mismatches this process was built to catch, not a specific client’s real workflow.

What I actually built

01

Requirements documents both sides could point back to

I sat with stakeholders to turn what they were asking for into a spec a delivery team could actually build against, the one document both sides could point to when scope got fuzzy halfway through a project.

02

Catching the gap before the client did

Before sign-off, I checked what shipped against what the BRD actually specified: missing steps, mismatched logic, scope that had quietly slipped, while there was still time to fix it.

03

Backing recommendations with numbers, not opinions

I pulled and modeled 7 months of sales data in SQL and Excel, so my process recommendations were backed by what the numbers actually showed, not just what stakeholders assumed.

Fixing the reporting layer, too

Requirements work wasn’t the only gap I closed at Radiance. Teams were pulling cross-functional sales and marketing numbers by hand for every reporting cycle, so I wrote SQL queries to consolidate that data into automated Tableau dashboards instead, self-service access that cut reporting cycles from 6 hours to under 90 minutes. One of the insights that came out of the cleaner data surfaced $180K+ in unrealized revenue, which changed how leadership prioritized the next quarter. I also ran a statistical comparison of vendor weather-forecasting error in Tableau to settle which vendor was actually most accurate, cutting the company’s associated expenditure.
